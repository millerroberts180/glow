4. WHAT GLOW DOES TO YOUR DISPLAYS

4.1 Glow changes the settings of the displays connected to your Mac. It sends commands to external
monitors over DDC/CI, the standard by which a computer controls a monitor through its video cable, to
change their brightness, contrast, volume, input, power and other settings they report. It changes the
brightness of your Mac's built-in display, and of Apple displays, through macOS. It can also dim any
display's picture in software, by changing the color tables macOS uses for that display. With your
permission (Accessibility), it takes over your keyboard's brightness keys to do this.

4.2 Each monitor's maker decides how it carries out DDC/CI commands, and some monitors do not follow
the standard. A monitor may ignore a command, carry out a different one, switch to another input or
into standby, or keep a setting after Glow quits. Glow asks before it switches a monitor's input or
power or restores its factory or color settings, but it cannot know how every monitor, cable, dock or
adapter will respond. If a display ends up in a state you do not want, its own buttons and on-screen
menu can change it back.

4.3 You are responsible for the settings you change, for the displays you use Glow with, and for
following their makers' instructions. Glow is not designed for displays where an unexpected change,
such as a screen going dark or switching input, could put a person or property at risk, and you must
not use it with them.
